What to notice

  • Slope = marks gained per extra hour; intercept = predicted marks at 0 hours.
  • MAE averages the error sizes; MSE averages the squared errors, so big misses count much more.
  • LinearRegression finds the line with the smallest MSE. You can sometimes beat its MAE, but never its MSE.

What to notice

  • Squaring the errors makes one far-away point pull the whole line towards it.
  • The shaded band is where we had data. Outside it, the line is a guess about a world we never saw.
  • Lines continue forever; real limits (100 marks, 24 hours a day) do not.

What R² means: squares, not magic

R² compares your line with the laziest model, a flat line at the average. Each error is drawn as a square: R² = 1 − (your squares) ÷ (the flat line's squares).

The flat line: always predict the average

Your line

R²

What to notice

  • At tilt 0 your line is the flat line: R² = 0.
  • The best line (tilt 1) has the smallest total square area, so the highest R².
  • A line worse than the flat line has more square area: R² becomes negative.
